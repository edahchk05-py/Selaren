# Principles

These principles govern product, sales, and implementation. When a request conflicts with them, refuse the request and escalate to the founder.

## 1. Revenue over features

Build only what helps a clinic convert inquiries into appointments and revenue. Extra capability is not progress.

## 2. Customer evidence over assumptions

Do not ship large work because it “seems obvious.” Confirm the problem in real clinics first.

## 3. Working product over beautiful prototypes

A used inbox that books appointments beats an unused demo.

## 4. One painful problem over ten mediocre problems

The painful problem is inquiry-to-appointment leakage. Do not dilute it.

## 5. Human support before unnecessary automation

Front-desk takeover is required. Autonomy is not the goal. Do not replace judgment with unattended AI.

## 6. Sell before scaling

In-person sales and paid (or measured) pilots come before growth tactics and extra markets.

## 7. Build what customers repeatedly need

One clinic asking once is a note. Repeated need across clinics is a candidate for the product.

## 8. Never become a generic AI wrapper

Do not compete as “ChatGPT for clinics.” Conversion outcomes are the product.

## 9. Never become a clinic PMS

No medical records, odontogram, CNSS/CNOPS, accounting, payroll, inventory, or clinic operations suites.

## 10. Stay obsessed with the inquiry-to-appointment conversion loop

If a decision does not improve capture, response, qualification, booking, deposit, follow-up, show-up, or measurement of that loop, it is out of scope.

## Authority

The founder owns customer discovery, sales, product direction, customer relationships, positioning, and business strategy.

Cursor is an implementation tool. It must not make major product or business decisions autonomously.

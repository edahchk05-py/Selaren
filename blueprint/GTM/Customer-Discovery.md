# Customer Discovery

Selaren is built on this loop:

**Research → Customer discovery → Problem validation → Product specification → Smallest possible MVP → Real customer → Measure results → Customer feedback → Iterate → Repeat**

Do not build large features based purely on founder assumptions.

## Purpose

Confirm, in real clinics:

- that inquiry-to-appointment leakage exists
- where it happens (response, qualification, booking, follow-up, deposit, show-up)
- how painful it is financially
- whether Selaren’s MVP would change the outcome

## Who to speak with

- clinic owner / decision-maker
- receptionist, secretary, or front desk

Observe the workflow. Do not interview only for opinions.

## What to observe

How inquiries arrive:

- WhatsApp
- Instagram
- website
- phone
- other channels the clinic actually uses

What happens after:

- speed of first response
- missed or unanswered messages
- qualification (or lack of it)
- how booking actually happens
- whether unconverted inquiries are followed up
- whether deposits are taken
- what the front desk uses today (notes, personal WhatsApp, paper, other tools)

## What to measure in discovery and pilots

- response time
- inquiries
- qualified inquiries
- appointments
- deposits
- follow-ups
- conversion

The North Star remains deposited consultations generated through Selaren.

## Evidence over assumptions

A repeated operational need across clinics can become product work.

A single request, a competitor feature, or a founder preference is not sufficient.

## Out of scope for discovery

Do not use discovery to justify:

- PMS, records, odontogram, CNSS/CNOPS
- accounting, payroll, inventory
- voice AI or autonomous agents
- other verticals or countries

If those topics come up, record them. Do not expand the company around them.

## Authority

The founder owns customer discovery, sales, and product direction.

Cursor may help organize notes or specs when asked. It must not conclude “the market wants X” and start building.
